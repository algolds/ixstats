## The One Addition I'd Make Beyond Our Previous Discussions: Intent as a Graph, Not a List

This is the feature I think would elevate IxStates beyond existing nation sims.

Don't store intents as a flat queue. Store them as a **directed graph**.

For example:

```text
National Vision
└── Become a Global Maritime Power
    ├── Modernize the Navy
    │   ├── Expand Shipbuilding Industry
    │   ├── Recruit Naval Personnel
    │   └── Adopt Blue Water Doctrine
    ├── Secure Sea Lanes
    │   ├── Sign Regional Defense Pact
    │   └── Build Overseas Naval Base
    └── Expand Maritime Economy
        ├── Port Modernization
        ├── Merchant Marine Subsidies
        └── Fisheries Reform
```

Now the government isn't just tracking "active tasks." It's managing an evolving strategic program where completing one commitment unlocks, blocks, or reshapes others. The graph itself becomes the living representation of the country's ambitions and gives every future subsystem—from AI flavor generation to ThinkPages to analytics—a common structure to build upon. I think this graph-based model is what transforms the Intent Engine from a workflow system into the true operating system for MyCountry.

I think this is actually the most important PRD in the entire MyCountry ecosystem.

# The Long-Term Vision: The Executive Dashboard as the Government Operating System

The Executive Dashboard should not feel like a homepage—it should feel like the operating system for governing a nation.

By the time a veteran player reaches year 2050, they shouldn't think in terms of "opening Politics" or "opening Diplomacy." They should begin every session on the dashboard, read the executive briefing, review their government's priorities, approve recommendations, convene meetings where necessary, and then dive into individual systems only when deeper involvement is required.

The Executive Dashboard becomes the conductor, while the Intent Engine provides direction and the Meeting System provides deliberation. Together, those three systems form the executive triad that everything else in MyCountry revolves around:

```text
Executive Dashboard
"What needs my attention?"

        ↓

Intent Engine
"What are we trying to accomplish?"

        ↓

Meeting System
"How should the government accomplish it?"

        ↓

Commitments

        ↓

"How is the administration progressing?"

        ↓

Executive Dashboard
"Here's what changed—and what now requires your leadership."
```

That closed loop turns the dashboard from a collection of widgets into the central command experience of MyCountry, reinforcing the philosophy that the player isn't managing abstract numbers—they're leading a living government through an evolving national story.

The current Elections flow revolves around scheduling and simulating elections with algorithmic modifiers, while Diplomacy revolves around CRUD for embassies, missions, treaties, and AI-generated scenarios. ## The Biggest Change I'd Make

If I were redesigning MyCountry today, I would literally remove the top-level pages named **Politics** and **Diplomacy**.

Not because those concepts disappear—but because they stop being isolated applications.

Instead, the Executive Dashboard becomes the entry point. The player starts with their briefing, declares or reviews national intents, convenes the necessary meetings, and then naturally moves into whichever executive domain is relevant. If today's priority is a housing reform, they flow into legislative meetings. If it's a regional security concern, they flow into foreign affairs and defense. If it's inflation, they enter the Economic Council.

The player's mental model changes from:

> "I'm opening the Diplomacy page."

to:

> "Today's government agenda requires a Foreign Affairs Summit."

That's a subtle UX shift, but it's a profound philosophical one. Politics, diplomacy, defense, and economics stop competing to be the "main feature" and instead become different expressions of the same governing process you've been defining with the Intent Engine and Meeting System. I think that's the design that will make MyCountry feel like a single, cohesive executive simulation rather than a collection of excellent but disconnected nation-sim subsystems.
