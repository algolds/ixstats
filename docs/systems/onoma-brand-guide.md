# 01 — Brand Foundation

## The Brand

**⟨ONOMA⟩** is a linguistic engine for creating, modeling, and evolving language.

Onoma began with names, but names are only the visible output of something deeper: a linguistic system.

A language has rules.

It has sounds, structures, patterns, histories, exceptions, and cultural meaning.

**Onoma gives creators the machinery to build those systems.**

---

## The Core Position

> **Onoma is not a name generator.**  
> **It is the linguistic engine behind the name.**

This distinction is foundational to the brand.

Markov generation is one technology inside Onoma.  
Phonotactics is one system.  
Sound change is one process.  
Lexicons are one layer.  
Speech synthesis is one interface.  

Together, they form a **linguistic engine**.

# 03 — Brand Thesis

## Language is a system.

A name is never simply a string of letters.

It emerges from:

**sound → structure → pattern → vocabulary → culture → history**

Onoma models that process.

A creator can define the sounds a language permits, establish the structures those sounds can occupy, construct a lexicon, apply historical transformations, and ultimately generate the names and words that naturally emerge from the system.

### The Onoma Thesis

> **Good linguistic creation does not begin with words. It begins with the system that makes those words possible.**

This is the intellectual foundation of the product.

# 06 — Audience

## Primary: Worldbuilders
People creating nations, cultures, fictional histories, cities, dynasties, organizations, civilizations, and fictional languages. They want consistency, depth, and believability.

## Secondary: Conlangers
Users interested in deliberately constructed languages. They care about phonology, phonotactics, morphology, lexicons, historical development, linguistic naturalism, and sound change. This audience should recognize that Onoma is **serious software**, not merely a generator.

## Tertiary: Linguistics & Language Enthusiasts
People interested in historical linguistics, phonetics, computational linguistics, language evolution, writing systems, and language analysis.

## Future: Creators & Developers
APIs, language datasets, procedural language systems, game development tools, narrative tools, simulation systems, and generative media. The brand must remain broad enough to accommodate this future.

# 09 — The Brand Voice

1. **Say less.** Communicate like sophisticated software. Avoid explaining everything immediately. Use hierarchy and whitespace. Allow the interface to reveal complexity progressively.
2. **Be precise.** Prefer *"Generate a language from phonological constraints"* over *"Create amazing, unique, realistic-sounding languages with the power of AI!"*
3. **Be confident.** Prefer *"Language, engineered"* over *"A new way to maybe help you create better names."*
4. **Be intellectually curious.** Use language that invites exploration (*"What happens when a language evolves?"*, *"Explore the sound system"*, *"Model the language"*, *"Trace the change"*).
5. **Never oversell the technology.** Do not make Markov models sound like artificial general intelligence. Technical credibility is more valuable than hype.

# 10 — Vocabulary

* **Preferred**: `engine`, `system`, `model`, `language`, `linguistic`, `phonology`, `lexicon`, `sound`, `evolve`, `construct`, `generate`, `explore`, `define`, `transform`, `derive`.
* **Use Carefully**: `generator` (acceptable when describing a specific feature, e.g. *Place Name Generator*), `AI` (use only when technically accurate), `realistic` (prefer *linguistically coherent* or *structurally consistent*).
* **Avoid**: `magic`, `magical`, `fantasy name machine`, `AI-powered magic`, `unlimited possibilities`, `epic`, `immersive`, `next-generation`, `revolutionary`, `game-changing`.

# 17 — Linguistic Vocabulary & Parameter Abstraction

A foundational rule of the Onoma product design language:

> **Expose the linguistic concept. Hide the implementation.**

The Onoma UI separates conceptual meaning from statistical mechanics across three distinct vocabulary layers:

| Layer | Audience | Terminology Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **User / Creative** | Worldbuilders, authors, creators | **Pattern Depth**, Variation, Sound Profile, Corpus, Vocabulary |
| **Linguistic / Analytical** | Conlangers, linguists | Phonotactics, N-grams, Phoneme Distribution, Lexical Diversity, Sound Shifts |
| **Implementation / Code** | Engine developers | `markovOrder`, `transitionMatrix`, `nGramSize`, `samplingTemperature` |

---

### Canonical Parameter: Pattern Depth

* **Definition**: *The depth of linguistic context used to model and generate forms.*
* **Behavioral Scale & Chromatic Thought Levels**:

| Depth | Tier | Color Code | Hex | Creative Outcome |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | **Fluid** | **Cyan** | `#06b6d4` | Broad linguistic patterns; high phonetic variation and exploratory sound combinations |
| **2** | **Organic** *(Recommended)* | **Azure** | `#0091ff` | Natural linguistic cadence; optimal conlang sweet spot balancing novelty & cohesion |

| **4** | **Strict** | **Amber** | `#f59e0b` | High pattern constraints; closely preserves literal word structures from training data |
