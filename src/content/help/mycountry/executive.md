## What directives are

A directive is a national goal you set yourself, such as "boost manufacturing", "cut the deficit" or "improve public health". It is the player-driven counterpart to [national issues](/help/gameplay/national-issues): issues come to you, directives come from you. Directives live on the Executive page ([/mycountry/executive](/mycountry/executive)); the **Declare Directive** button in the MyCountry header takes you there.

## The Executive page

- **Status strip.** How many weekly slots you have left (or the countdown until the next one), your executive bandwidth free and in use, and how many directives are in force.
- **New directive.** The four-step composer below.
- **In force.** Your active directives.
- **History.** Completed and abandoned directives.

## How to declare a directive

1. **Goal.** Search, type your own goal in plain language, or browse the presets (66 presets across 8 domains). **Suggest one** picks a goal for you. Foreign-policy goals are refused; choose **Change goal** and use [Diplomacy](/help/mycountry/diplomacy) for those.
2. **Approach.** Choose **Measured**, **Moderate** or **Extreme**, plus a **Broker deal** when a satisfied power broker unlocks one. Each shows its bandwidth cost, how likely it is to be accepted, and its resistance risk.
3. **Projected impact.** Read the effects in plain English, including any GDP effect phased in over one IxTime year.
4. **Review and declare.** Check your bandwidth before and after and your remaining slots, then declare.

## Managing directives in force

Each directive in **In force** shows **Executing** (still holding bandwidth) or **In force**, and a resistance progress bar if opposition issues are linked to it. From its card you can:

- **Follow up** with a related directive.
- **Respond** to a linked resistance issue (opens the issue brief).
- **Complete** it. This stays disabled until its resistance issues are resolved.
- **Abandon** it. You're asked to confirm; abandoning releases its bandwidth but not its weekly slot.

Directives are completed or abandoned here, not from the agenda.

## History

**History** lists completed and abandoned directives with their recorded before → after effects. Filter by All, Completed or Abandoned, and use **Declare again** to reuse a goal.

## Limits

- **3 per IxTime week.** The weekly cap counts every directive you declared in the last 7 IxTime days (3.5 real days), whatever happened to it since. Completing or abandoning a directive frees its bandwidth but not its slot; the slot opens again 7 IxTime days after you declared it. Drafts you never declared don't use a slot. When all three slots are used, the status strip and the MyCountry header show a countdown to the next one.
- **Resistance.** Active directives make related national issues more likely to come up, representing groups that oppose or support you. A directive with open resistance issues can't be completed until they're resolved.

## What a directive changes

- **Economy and infrastructure goals** add a GDP effect that phases in over one IxTime year (six real months) and then stays. Its size depends on the approach.
- **Defense goals** raise stability.
- **Budget lines.** Some approaches shift your department budget allocations a little.
- **The record.** Every directive is logged to your nation's history and change log, and completing one produces a draft summary post for ThinkPages.

## Executive bandwidth

Executive bandwidth is how much work your government can carry at once: the time and attention of its civil service. It grows with your population and government effectiveness. The status strip shows how much is free, and its bar is split by what is using it. Select the help button beside it for your live breakdown.

These use bandwidth:

- **Government staff.** Your government components, all the time (15% less when the Technocrats are satisfied).
- **Policies.** Every policy in effect, until you repeal it.
- **Directives.** For their execution week.
- **Recon meetings.** 20 each, while in progress.
- **Delegated issues.** 15 each, for 5 IxTime days.

Directives use bandwidth while they're executing, for up to one IxTime week:

| Approach    | Bandwidth used |
| ----------- | -------------- |
| Measured    | 5              |
| Moderate    | 12             |
| Extreme     | 25             |
| Broker deal | 8              |

Completing or abandoning a directive frees its bandwidth early. Going over doesn't block you, but recon and policy previews become less reliable until bandwidth frees up.

Executive bandwidth was called executive bandwidth before.

## Tips

- Start with Measured approaches while you learn how your nation reacts.
- Keep a slot free for emergencies, such as a crisis issue that needs a follow-up.
- If an issue keeps coming back, turn it into a directive from its brief.
