## Money in, forces out

Your forces come from your **defense budget**: every Defense department line of your [National budget](/help/mycountry/economy) added together. A nation without a Defense department uses the Defense spending set when it was founded, then its government form's usual share, and as a last resort 2% of GDP.

Each month on your realm's clock, the budget is split into five lines, and each line buys only its own thing:

| Line | What it buys |
|---|---|
| Personnel | Pay for active personnel and refresher training for reservists |
| Operations and maintenance | Training, equipment upkeep and supply |
| Procurement | Equipment orders, and generic equipment with whatever is left |
| Research and development | Keeps your industry near the newest designs |
| Infrastructure | Bases and fortifications |

So a force with plenty of pay but no upkeep money has the people but lets them go untrained; a force with lots of equipment and too few people can't crew it. Nothing changes overnight: recruiting closes about 5% of the gap a month, training takes most of a year to build, and a maintenance backlog takes years to clear. A date jump in your realm moves all of this forward by the time that passes.

**Personnel.** Each person costs pay tied to your GDP per head: a volunteer costs about 2.2 times it a year, a conscript about 0.6 times. The personnel line decides how many you can pay; your population decides how many will serve. If your force is bigger than its pay, it shrinks toward what you can afford.

**Equipment.** Equipment is a stock that wears out: land equipment loses about 10% of its value a year, ships about 6%, aircraft about 9%, faster on alert or on operations. Procurement money replaces it. Catalog equipment adds quality: newer designs fight better, and every design loses about 3% a year against the newest ones.

## Readiness and C-levels

Every branch is rated on four areas, each from 0 to 100%:

- **Personnel fill**: posts filled against what the branch's funded strength and equipment crews need.
- **Equipment on hand**: equipment held against what its people are organised around. Mass conscription with no kit scores low.
- **Equipment condition**: how much of the equipment works. It follows maintenance money, slowly.
- **Training**: how well the force is trained. It rises when funded and fades over about nine months when not. Conscripts train to a lower ceiling than volunteers.

Readiness leans on the **weakest** area, so the page always names it with what lifts it. Readiness is shown as a rating:

| Rating | Readiness | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| C-1 | 90% or more | Fully ready |
| C-2 | 80 to 89% | Mostly ready |
| C-3 | 70 to 79% | Partly ready |
| C-4 | under 70% | Not ready |

Analysts know the four areas as the P, S, R and T levels.

## Combat power

A branch's combat power grows with its equipment and its people (with diminishing returns, so piling up more of one helps less and less), multiplied by readiness, equipment quality, support and enablers, and your nation's **human factor**. Combat power is what battles and threat assessments use, so a large force that is untrained or badly kept counts for less.

## The levers

### Budget split

Move any line and the others adjust so the split always adds up to 100%. Before you save, the card shows what the split buys: how many people pay supports, the strength your force moves toward, and how much of maintenance and training is funded. Three presets:

- **Professional**: a smaller, well-trained and well-kept volunteer force.
- **Territorial conscript**: a large force of conscripts and reserves for home defense.
- **Rearm**: half the budget on new equipment while the force holds its size.

Under 20% on procurement, your equipment wears out faster than you replace it.

### Manpower

- **All-volunteer**: costly per person and slow to recruit, but trains to the highest standard.
- **Conscription**: cheap and large, and fills the reserve fast, but trains to a lower ceiling.
- **Mixed**: both side by side.

Conscript service runs 6 to 18 months; longer service keeps more conscripts under arms. Everyone who leaves joins the reserve (every conscript, half of volunteers) for the years you set.

### Doctrine

**Defensive**, **balanced** or **expeditionary**. Doctrine sets how fast reservists report: fastest when defensive, slowest when expeditionary.

### Alert level

| Level | Upkeep | Wear | Takes effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peacetime | normal | normal | at once |
| Elevated | +15% | +20% | after 7 days |
| High | +40% | +50% | after 14 days |
| War | double | triple | after 30 days |

Raising the alert lets training climb higher but costs more upkeep and wears equipment faster, and it only takes effect after its days pass on your realm's clock. Lowering it is instant. Other nations can see a high alert.

### Upkeep priority

Splits the operations and maintenance line by need, or favours keeping equipment working, or keeping the force trained.

### Production surge

Doubles domestic production capacity over 24 months; domestic orders cost 30% more while it runs.

### Branch plans

Each branch has an effort weight (its share of the national effort, scaled so all branches add up to 100%) and an optional strength cap below what the budget funds.

## Mobilisation

You can call up part of a branch's reserve. Reservists report gradually, not at once: about a quarter within 3 days, most within two weeks and nearly all within 60 days (faster under a defensive doctrine, slower under an expeditionary one). They come back at reserve-level training, so readiness can dip while they settle in.

Their pay is not in the defense budget: it is an **emergency supplement** on top of it, so mobilising raises your defense spending as a share of GDP for as long as they serve. **Demobilise** sends everyone in that branch home at once and stops the supplement.
