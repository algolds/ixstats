[IDEOLOGY] Pan-Cronanism
Categories: IXWB, Political_ideologies
Snippet:
'''Pan-Cronanism''', a movement which has recently gained momentum in the 2020's, is the political ideology calling for the unification of states on the continent of [[Crona]] into a larger political entity, commonly in the form of supranational organizations, but also rarely in form of one of several unified Cronan superstates. The movement is most popular in southern Crona, with smaller movements elsewhere on the continent.

==Origins==
The Pan-Cronan movement emerged after the end of the [[Great Cronan War]] in the mid-late 1950s. It originally began as a movement of peace, intended to call for peaceful resolution to any further conflicts that could escalate into another Great War. As time went on, however, the movement grew into one that sought to join together the continent of Crona in some respect of unity and cooperation. Kronatan congressman [[Gregor Friedrich Mandel]] coined the term ''Pan-Cronanism'' in his 1961 speech to the [[Kronatan Legislature|Bundeskongress]], saying that "It is a necessary idea of brotherly cooperation between all the states of Crona to rebuild the relationships shaken by the war."

'''Organicism''' is a political and moral philosophy based on the existence of organically developed, interconnected societies and public life as an expression of these concepts. Organicists argue that all human persons are social animals and, consequently, humans are created to be governed and live in society. Accordingly, society is not created but is an organic, developing institution based around the organically developed set of social relationships to which all individuals are subject; since this is the case, it is in society's interests to ensure the common good, defined as "the sum total of social conditions which allow people, either as groups or as individuals, to reach their fulfillment more fully and more easily". It includes the economic policies and movements associated with such systems. Social ownership can be public, collective, cooperative, or of equity. 

While no single definition encapsulates the many types of principles, social ownership is the one common element that has been higlighted in the 21st century. 

Historically Pelaxian Socialists disagreed about the degree to which social control or regulation of the economy is necessary, how far society should intervene and whether government, particularly existing government, is the correct vehicle for change. Pelaxian Socialism lines of thought have been divided in non-market and market forms. Marx's view of the materialist progression, however, has caused divisions among Urcean Marxists since its inception; writing as a continental [[Sarpedon|Sarpedonian]], it was not clear to Marxists of the 19th century through today if this straight-forward model is applicable to Urcea. This fundamental disagreement led to the division of Marxists within Urcea between the "Traditionalist Marxists" and the "Particular Marxists".

Disurbanism posits that the {{wp|city}} (and in many formulations, the {{wp|town}}) is a harmful and unnecessary formation and a poor way to organise human life, and that cities and towns as currently understood should be abandoned in favour of more geographically distributed patterns of settlement. Most disurbanists believe that while cities once served legitimate purposes, they have since been rendered obsolete by modern advances in communications and transportation technology, and that the utilitarian benefits of cities no longer justify the social, economic, and political costs of urbanocentrism.

Nolanism is generally opposed to [[Crown Liberalism]] and views the emancipation of the individual and enshrinement of {{wp|civil rights}} at the center of Urcean society as its chief aims. Adherents of the ideology also denounce {{Wp|socialism}} and various left-wing movements, feeling that they often make the monarchy a politically distracting issue while making their civil rights-oriented goals more easily subject to {{wp|strawman}} dismissals. The ideology is named for Linar Orens-Nolan, a onetime [[National Pact (Urcea)|National Pact]] member of the [[Conshilía Daoni]] and mid-20th century thought leader.

'''Kaidoism''' ([[Pomorskitong]]: ''Kaidoézm'') is a [[Nasastan|Nasastani]] political ideology and movement based on the ideas of Načelnik [[Kréstofer Kaido]] (1800 - 1839). Emerging in the early 19th century, it represents one of the principal ideological offshoots of [[Resistanceism]], often described as Nasastan's own internally developed variant of socialist thought. Kaidoism emphasizes the pursuit of [[Final egalitarianism|final egalitarianism]], the gradual equalization and standardization of all facets of social and economic life under a state-managed order. Although initially associated with Kaido’s rule in the 1830s, the term Kaidoism did not enter common usage until the tenure of Načelnik [[Kats Kontor]] (1844 - 1849), whose public identification with Kaido's legacy led to its formal recognition as a distinct ideological tradition within Nasastani politics.

The term Kaidoism derives from the surname of Kréstofer Kaido, Načelnik of Nasastan between 1829 and 1839. The suffix -ism (''-ézm'' in Pomorskitong) was first applied during the late 1840s by political commentators under the [[Aristocratic Sovereignty of Nasastan]] to describe Kontor's attempts to restore elements of Kaido’s program.

'''Guided democracy''', also called '''directed democracy''' and '''managed democracy''', is a ''de jure'' {{wp|democracy|democratic}} {{wp|government|government}} that nonetheless functions as a ''de facto'' {{wp|Authoritarianism|authoritarian regime}} or, in some cases, as an outright {{wp|Autocracy|autocratic regime}}. Such forms of {{wp|Hybrid regime|hybrid regimes}} are legitimised by elections, but these elections do not serve to change the {{wp|State (polity)|state}}'s government in any meaningful way but rather are deemed as {{wp|sham election|shams}} designed to keep up the appearance of a {{wp|liberal democracy}}.

In a guided democratic system, elections are directly controlled and manipulated by the government; people are permitted to exercise their democratic rights, but the government ensures that voters cannot change public policy. While they ostensibly follow basic democratic principles, they generally are beholden to a variety of authoritarianism to keep democracy "on a leash". '''Kallistocracy''' ([[Pomorskitong]]: ''Kallistokracéa'', literally ''government for the best condition'') is a political and philosophical doctrine originating in [[Nasastan]] that holds that the primary purpose of the state is to preserve the most desirable form of ordinary human life for the majority of the population. Rather than defining the state through sovereignty, liberty, or divine authority, Kallistocracy argues that government exists to maximize collective convenience, social stability, and interpersonal trust while minimizing unnecessary struggle. The doctrine constitutes one of the central political expressions of [[Zmoric Philosophy]] and the [[Lakhe'žic]] principle of 'easy life', serving as the dominant ideological framework of the modern Nasastani state.
